You will make a **facade of 20 × 12 m**, divided into panels of 1 m, with a **vertical sun fin in each panel**. The
fins near a point turn open; the ones far from it stay closed. Seven nodes, no formulas.

## Before you start: two gestures

- **Add a node:** double-click on an empty spot of the canvas, or press **Tab**. Type its name and press **Enter**.
- **Connect two nodes:** drag from the small circle on the **right** of a card (what the node gives) to an input of
  another card.

## Step by step

<Steps>

1. **The facade.** Add a **Rectangle**. In its card, write **width** `20`, **height** `12` and choose **plane:
   elevation**. A rectangle standing up appears in the view. If you do not see it, turn on the eye of the card.

2. **The panels.** Add a **Grid** and connect the Rectangle to its input **rectangle**. Write **cell width** `1` and
   **cell height** `1`. The cable now reads `row 12 × column 20 = 240`: 240 panels.

3. **The centre of each panel.** Add a **Centre** and connect the Grid to its input **geometry**. You get 240 points,
   one in the middle of each panel.

4. **The point that attracts.** Add a **Point** with **x** `6`, **y** `0` and **z** `7`: a spot on the facade, 6 m
   from the left and 7 m up.

5. **An angle for each panel.** Add an **Attractor**. Connect the Centre to **where** and the Point to
   **attractors**. Write **reach** `8`, **near** `90` and **far** `0`. Each panel now has an angle: 90° right next to
   the point, 0° at 8 m or farther.

6. **The fin.** Add a **Box** with **width** `0.4`, **depth** `0.05` and **height** `1`: a thin vertical blade.

7. **A fin in every panel.** Add a **Place**. Connect the Box to **geometry**, the Centre to **at** and the
   Attractor to **rotation**. 240 fins appear: open around the point, closed far from it.

</Steps>

## Now play with it

This is where parametric design pays off. Change any number and the whole facade answers:

- Move the **Point**: change its **x** or **z** and watch the opening travel across the facade.
- Change the **reach** of the Attractor: a small reach opens only a few fins, a large one opens most of them.
- Swap **near** and **far**: the fins close around the point and open everywhere else.
- Change the **cell width** of the Grid to `0.5`: twice as many fins, without touching anything else.

## Tips

- **See only what matters.** Turn off the eye of the Rectangle, the Grid and the Centre, so the view shows only the
  fins.
- **Fins in front of the facade.** The fins sit on the plane of the facade. To bring them 30 cm forward, put a
  **Move** between the Centre and the Place with **vector** `0; -0.3; 0`.
- **Save** your project with **File → Save**. Tensela projects end in `.tela`.

## What you just learnt

- A design is a chain of small steps, each one a node. [Nodes and cables](/manual/key-ideas/nodes-and-cables/)
- The Grid made a list of 240 panels, and every node after it worked on all 240 at once.
  [Lists with names](/manual/key-ideas/named-lists/)
- The Attractor made each panel different on purpose.
  [Conditions and attractors](/manual/key-ideas/conditions-and-attractors/)
