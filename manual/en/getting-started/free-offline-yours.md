Three things worth knowing before you start:

- **Free:** you can use Tensela for anything, at no cost.
- **Offline:** it works entirely on your computer.
- **Yours:** what you make with it belongs to you.

![A table on a building site, with a laptop working without signal](/images/manual/sketches/offline-en.svg)

## Free: what the licence lets you do

Tensela is **free software**: it costs nothing, and its licence is the
[GNU General Public License, version 3 or later](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.html) (GPL-3.0), the licence
of many well-known programs of Linux. In plain words:

| You can | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Use it for anything** | Your studies, your professional work, paid commissions, your classes. Without paying, without an account, with no time limit. |
| **Install it on all your computers** | And give copies to whoever you want. |
| **See how it is made** | Its source code is public, so anyone can check what it does. |
| **Change it** | And share your changes, under the same licence. |

**The one condition:** if you share Tensela, or a version you changed, you share its source code too, with the same
licence. That way Tensela, and every version made from it, stays open for everyone.

Like all free software, Tensela comes **without warranty**: it is offered as it is. Check the results that carry
responsibility with a professional; [Bar analysis](/manual/nodes/simulation/#bar-analysis), for instance, is a first
check, not a structural calculation. The licence, the warranty and privacy, all on one page:
[Licence, warranty and privacy](/legal/).

## Your projects are yours

> The licence covers the program, not what you make with it.

- Your **projects** (`.tela`), the **files you export** (OBJ, DXF, SVG, PDF…) and your **images** are yours.
- Use them, sell them, publish them as you like.
- You do not need to mention Tensela, although it is always appreciated.

## Offline: everything on your computer

> Tensela never sends your projects anywhere.

- **Tensela itself does not connect to the internet.** No account, no sign-in, no updates in the background, no
  advertising, no statistics of how you use it.
- **The sun** is calculated with astronomical formulas and a list of cities inside the program, or the latitude and
  longitude you type. No weather file to download.
- **The [Code](/manual/nodes/values/#code) node** is made not to reach the network: Tensela checks the code before
  running it, and stops it if it tries to open files, reach the network or start other programs.
- **Your files stay where you save them.** Tensela does not upload them anywhere. (An extension can, only if its card
  says so in amber.)
- **You only need internet** to download Tensela, this manual and the new versions, when you decide.

Why it matters:

- It works **in a site office without signal**, on a trip, or in a classroom with a poor connection.
- Your projects **stay with you**: nobody else sees them.
- Once it is on your computer, it **does not depend on any server** to keep working.
